How to Clean and Maintain Your Geyser for Maximum Efficiency

RV

Robert Vance

Master Heating Specialist at VoltFix Repairs

The Hard Truth About Scale Deposits

When hard water is heated inside a geyser, minerals like calcium and magnesium precipitate out. These minerals settle onto the heating element and base of the tank, forming a thick, chalky layer known as scale. This scale acts as an thermal insulator, forcing your heating element to run hotter and longer to reach target temperatures.

Step-by-Step Descaling Guide

  1. Isolate Power Breakers: Before opening any valves, ensure the dedicated DP switch at the distribution board is turned off. Verify terminals using a voltmeter.
  2. Shut Off Cold Water Input: Close the incoming cold water isolator valve to stop tank replenishment.
  3. Attach Drainage Hose: Connect a standard hose to the geyser drain outlet, and run the hose to a local bathroom floor drain.
  4. Open Safety Pressure Valve: Open the temperature pressure relief valve to allow air inside, starting the gravity-drainage process.
  5. Descale the Element: Unbolt the base flange, extract the mineral scale deposits, and clean out the magnesium anode rod. If corroded, replace both the anode rod and element immediately.
"Descaling water heaters once a year can double their functional lifespans and reduce monthly energy bills by up to 25%."

CRITICAL WARNING: Ground Fault Leakage

Never attempt electrical repairs on water heaters while power breakers are turned on. Water geysers represent high-amperage zones. Scaled elements are prone to micro-cracks that allow voltage leaks directly into bathing lines. Ground continuity audits must be performed with multimeters before returning to normal service.
